Explainable Web Access Control for Android

ChildSafe · Published research

Research Overview

Designed, built, and evaluated ChildSafe, an Android prototype that connects an Android agent, a Windows policy gateway, and a parent dashboard to show why websites were allowed or blocked. Wrote the published paper as its sole author.

Led the research, developed the Android parental-control prototype, and authored the published paper independently. Revised the classifier to analyse page text and explain blocking decisions after the first URL-keyword filter blocked a page warning about scams.

  • Word and character TF-IDF
  • Logistic regression
  • Uncertainty checks
  • Android access control

Motivation for ChildSafe

Explored ways to help parents recognise online risks, receive warnings, and discuss online safety with their children after seeing friends become involved in online gambling.

ChildSafe simplifies device pairing and offers preset blocking options alongside parent-defined website rules. The prototype scores website-content risk and records why access was blocked. A high-risk assessment can trigger an email alert to parents and temporarily lock Internet access on the child’s device.

Key Research Objectives

  • Classify public website text into seven categories and leave uncertain cases unresolved.
  • Record whether an access decision came from a parent-defined rule, a model-based block, or an uncertain assessment.
  • Test the classifier and check the prototype’s access-control and remote-management workflows.

Methodology

The classifier combines word- and character-level TF-IDF features with multiclass logistic regression. It uses confidence, margin, and similarity checks to decide when to withhold a label. The study used 140 AI-authored training texts, a held-out pilot set of 42 separately authored texts, and an additional 24-case review. The Android release checks used one Galaxy Tab, a Windows host, and local Wi-Fi.

Pilot Results

  • 41 of 42 texts (97.6%) received the expected top-ranked label before abstention. Macro F1 was 0.976.
  • The system accepted 34 of 42 texts after applying its uncertainty checks. All 34 accepted labels were correct, and the classifier abstained on eight texts.
  • In a controlled device scenario, a parent could review the decision, unlock the device, and restore web access.

Impact & Application

The prototype shows how text classification, parental rules, and a reviewable decision history can work together. The study was limited to authored text and one Android device. Accuracy on real websites and effectiveness in family use remain open questions.

Publication

ChildSafe Design and Preliminary Evaluation of an Explainable Web Access Control System for Android. International Journal of Scientific Research and Management, 24 October 2026. DOI 10.18535/ijsrm/v14i10.ec07.

A Bidirectional Digital Twin for Secure Freight Transportation

SecureSupply Twin · Cold-chain logistics

Research Overview

SecureSupply Twin connects a stationary freight model to a dashboard. Operators can see a disruption, compare the estimated costs of possible responses and check whether the device carried out their chosen action. Co-authored the INOVA exhibition entry.

Helped design the connection between the freight model and its dashboard. Assembled and soldered circuits and wrote code to transmit sensor readings and relay the model’s response to approved commands.

  • ESP32
  • Sensor integration
  • Bidirectional communication
  • Cost modelling

Key Research Objectives

  • Synchronize cargo temperature, weight, door status, and power readings with the dashboard.
  • Detect disruptions such as cooling failure, power loss, cargo weight loss, unexpected door openings, and warehouse unavailability.
  • Compare response options using estimated costs, then send operator-approved commands and verify their execution.

Methodology

Sensors send readings to an ESP32 controller. A decision engine compares available responses using cargo value, delay penalties, warehouse fees, and intervention costs. The dashboard sends approved commands back to the model and records device acknowledgement or timeout.

The evaluation plan compares the prototype with a monitoring-only dashboard in repeatable disruption scenarios. Planned measures include detection and response time, state agreement, command acknowledgement, and modelled cost.

Impact & Application

The prototype gives dispatchers a record from the original sensor event through the recommended action and device response. In an illustrative warehouse scenario, waiting was estimated at VND 1,500,000 and switching warehouses at VND 600,000. The VND 900,000 difference comes from the model. The prototype was built around a stationary freight model, and field trials remain future work.

Medication and Vital-Sign Monitoring at Home

MediCare Hub · Home-care prototype

Research Overview

MediCare Hub is a team-built home-care prototype that connects a mobile app, a seven-day pill dispenser and a vital-sign monitoring module. The system was designed for older adults following a medication schedule and for relatives helping them at home.

Led the research team and handled AI modelling and system integration. Wrote the code that connected the prescription-reading app, the dispenser, and the vital-sign sensors so caregivers could follow a single care routine across devices.

  • Prescription OCR
  • Stepper motor control
  • Infrared cup detection
  • Vital-sign monitoring

Key Research Objectives

  • Extract medication names and dosages from prescription images for review and scheduling.
  • Dispense scheduled doses and detect when the dose cup is removed.
  • Display heart rate, oxygen saturation, and temperature readings in a cloud-connected app.
  • Alert caregivers about missed doses or readings outside preset thresholds.

Methodology

The app uses optical character recognition (OCR) to read a prescription image and asks the user to confirm the details before scheduling. A stepper motor positions the medication carousel, and an infrared sensor detects whether the cup was removed. A separate monitoring module records vital signs for the app to display.

Impact & Application

The prototype brings dose schedules, dispenser activity, and vital-sign trends into one view. Cup removal is the recorded event. The project was developed as a home-care prototype, with clinical testing and telehealth integration as possible future work.
